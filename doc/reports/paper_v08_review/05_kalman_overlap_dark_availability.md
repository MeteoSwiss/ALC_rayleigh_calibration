### A.3 The two headline claims

**"physically consistent uncertainty propagation"** — Not supported by what the code does. (i) σ_m² is one empirical scalar per series (K1: residuals to a sinusoid; K2/K3: residuals to a rolling mean), so the propagated σ(t) is the same on a 3 %-uncertainty night as on a 25 %-uncertainty night; the per-night uncertainty of 5.1.1 (ensemble spread) plays no role — the author's own TODO says so (`cal_best_estimate.py:36-44`). (ii) σ_e,c², σ_e,t² are hand-set per type (K1) or one relative pair for every instrument type and magnitude (K3). (iii) σ² after an update is σ_a²(1−K) with no model-error term for the sinusoid misfit, and after a gap the variance grows linearly (random walk), which is "consistent" with the chosen state model, not with any physics of the instrument. (iv) In K3 the uncertainty is in normalised units rescaled by the series median (`kalman.py:205`). The honest wording is "a Kalman filter with empirically set measurement and process variances"; "physically consistent" should go.

**"robustness against outliers"** — Partly. K1 flags with ONE global IQR on raw values: a series with a 30–40 % seasonal cycle (Fig. 6) has an IQR wide enough that only gross outliers are flagged, and a real step (module swap) flags a whole era (which is the intended behaviour for the *last* module, but makes every earlier module "outliers"). K2 (Fig. 6) and K3 use a rolling window, which tolerates steps but also follows a drifting outlier cluster. The memory note records a 2.5× difference in outlier counts between the two screens on the same 21 CHM15k streams (73 global vs 179 rolling). The claim is defensible only if the paper names the screen and its window; "87 outliers" in Fig. 6 is a K2 (rolling, dropped) number, not a K1 number.

### B.4 Statistical / logical weaknesses

1. **Spearman on n = 10 with two influential points.** Leave-one-out: dropping Payerne → ρ = 0.58, dropping Uccle → 0.58 (p ≈ 0.08–0.10, not significant); dropping Sion → 0.82, Zeebrugge → 0.77. The rank correlation stands or falls with the two units the appendix set out to explain, which makes the "ranking agrees" argument partly circular; the three units with the largest positive baselines (Aosta, Lanzhot, Birkenes, +0.02…+0.066) have ratios 1.00–1.10, i.e. the relation is not monotonic across the full range either. Also the ratio on the y-axis is the Feb–Jun method-test ratio (Payerne 0.83) while the same unit's Mar–Oct ratio in A6-1 is 0.78; keep one definition or say why they differ. p = 0.03 should not be presented as a result on n = 10 — at most "consistent with".
2. **"% of molecular" normalisation** is used with three different denominators in A5/A6: US-Std-76 + two-way molecular transmission without WV at 1064.47 / 909.7 / 910.55 nm (A5-2, A6-2), CAMS-based molecular at 910.55 / 1064 nm (A6-1c, `molecular_iqr`), and with C_L = Rayleigh Kalman (CHM15k; biased low by the very dark being normalised, so the CHM15k dark/molecular is overstated by ~25 %) or cloud Kalman (CL61) — and A5-1 with a hard-coded 5e11 and a nominal 1e8. State one convention (wavelength, atmosphere, WV, altitude reference, constant) and use it throughout.
3. **Two hood products (v2 vs v3)** in one appendix (see table).
4. **"Lower bound" vs "negligible"** — the far-field offset is either a third of the effect (text) or negligible (code decision); it cannot be both.
5. **Cloud immunity** — true for a static dark by orders of magnitude, but the appendix's own opaque-cloud result (signal-dependent undershoot persisting 2 km above the cloud) shows the CL61 baseline is not static under strong returns; the sentence "its integration between 100 and 2400 m … makes it insensitive to it" should be limited to the static dark, and the "~0.001 % measured" needs a source.
6. **Payerne CL31 narrative:** "decreased steadily" is not what the six post-swap sessions show.
7. **Selection effect in 62 → 102 nights:** the +dark configuration passes QC on 40 more nights; the ratio 0.94 is computed on a different (larger) night sample than 0.78. Report the ratio on the 62 common nights as well.
