# Manuscript-level adversarial findings (own read of v0.8, independent of the code agents)

## A. Status / transparency (what is actually operational) - MAJOR
1. Which constants reach the distributed L2? Sect. 4.3 says lidar_constant_0 comes from "the monthly Rayleigh-calibration products"; CL31/CL51 fall back to 1e8, CL61 to 1.0. Nothing says that the cloud constants are applied in the hub. Sect. 3 says the CL31 "is calibrated by the manufacturer using liquid cloud signal (O'Connor 2004, Hopkin 2019)" - wrong attribution (it is the E-PROFILE service, not Vaisala) and misleading if the constant is not applied in L2. Sect. 6 validates "the daily Kalman best-estimate ... as published by the calibration monitoring service" (dashboard), which is not necessarily what a CEDA/GTS user receives. The repo's own ops notes (doc/OPERATIONS.md: ALC_OLDRAY_DIR = "v1.0 (old operational)"; CLAUDE.md: "L2 as distributed: CHM15k and CL61 = operational Rayleigh v1.0; CL31 = UNCALIBRATED (flat default 1e8)") indicate the hub applies v1.0 Rayleigh constants and no cloud constants, while the paper calls v2.0 "the operational E-Profile Rayleigh calibration". The paper must state, with a date: (a) which Rayleigh version is applied in the distributed L2, (b) whether cloud constants are applied or only published, (c) the plan/date of the switch.
2. Cloud aggregation is described three ways: Sect. 5.1.2 "aggregated monthly ... median of all monthly medians of the full multi-year record; inter-monthly SD = stability"; Appendix 3 flowchart "Daily median C -> Kalman-smoothed calibration constant"; Sect. 6 "daily Kalman best-estimate lidar constants ... liquid-cloud calibration for the Vaisala ceilometers". A multi-year median cannot be a real-time product nor track the CL31 optical-module swap handled in 6.3. Pick one and describe it once.
3. Sect. 5.1.3 only mentions Rayleigh constants as Kalman input; Fig. 2 shows "Rayleigh / cloud calibration -> Kalman filter". Say explicitly that the Kalman is applied to both (and with which parameters for cloud).

## D. Scientific / logical weaknesses a referee will raise
24. Sect. 6.2 circularity: the EARLINET 1064 nm elastic product is itself normalised to the molecular profile in an aerosol-free reference region (Klett-Fernald in the SCC), as is the CHM15k Rayleigh calibration. Agreement in the free troposphere is therefore partly by construction (and any difference in the molecular model - SCC radiosonde/standard atmosphere vs the CHM15k's reference - appears directly as a calibration difference). What the comparison really tests: transfer of the nightly constant to all hours, the profile shape (overlap, dark) and the aerosol-layer transmission handling. Say it, and weight the BL/aerosol-layer agreement (Leipzig +7.7 % in 1-2 km) accordingly.
25. Sect. 6.2: "The reported differences are therefore an upper bound on the Rayleigh calibration error" is not logically valid (two biased quantities can cancel). Rephrase as "consistent within the combined uncertainty".
26. Sect. 6.1 CHM15k/Mini-MPL cloud calibration is a straw man: the paper concedes that the generic screening "contains no [saturation] test" that Hopkin et al. used. Either implement a saturation rejection and show it still fails, or present the CHM15k cloud numbers as a demonstration of the operational configuration only, not as a method test.
27. Sect. 6.3 Payerne: the reference (CL61, cloud constant) carries its own altitude-dependent dark (App. 6: -7 to -28 % of molecular at 3-6 km), so the headline "+30.4 %" is not a CHM15k-only error; part of it is the reference profile biased low at altitude. State this where the number is introduced, not only in App. 6.
28. Conclusions: "the remaining discrepancies ... are dominated by range-dependent signal artefacts ... rather than by the calibration" contradicts App. 6, which shows the dark biases the operational Rayleigh CONSTANT of the Payerne CHM15k by ~20 % (a calibration error of the product). Reword: the dark distorts the profile AND biases the Rayleigh constant; the network-wide prevalence of such darks is unknown (give the limitation).
29. Conclusions: "comparisons with atmospheric composition and NWP products illustrate their relevance for model evaluation" - nothing of the kind is shown (Fig. 9 is an unexplained placeholder). Remove or add the analysis. "Substantially enhances ... compared with radiosonde networks" - no evidence given.
30. Sect. 1: "there was no operational network able to deliver the vertical distribution of aerosols in real time" - the DWD ceilometer network did observe the Eyjafjallajoekull plume in near real time (Flentje et al. 2010; Emeis et al. 2011). Soften ("no harmonised European network").
31. Sect. 5.1.3 seasonal cycle attributed to "an imperfect correction of the temperature on the instrument" on the basis of unshown housekeeping parameters; cite Buxmann (2024) and Van Hove & Diemoz (2024) (both in the list, both uncited, both co-author work) and Bellini et al. (2024); exclude the molecular-reference contribution (finding 14) and the overlap-temperature cross-talk before attributing it entirely to the instrument.
32. Sect. 5.1.3 Kalman: the sinusoidal model is fitted to the whole record (non-causal) - say how the NRT extension is fitted (past data only?). The measurement variance is one scalar from the fit residuals, so the per-night 2 sigma uncertainty of 5.1.1 is not used: the claim "physically consistent uncertainty propagation" is overstated (co-author comment "how does sigma_m compare with the 5.1.1 uncertainty?" is still open). Thresholds for constant vs sinusoid, sigma_e,c^2, sigma_e,t^2 and the "instrument-specific default constants" are not given (a parameter table was requested by a co-author). The global IQR outlier test on a series with a 30-40 % seasonal cycle will flag seasonal extremes; the filter does not detect steps (optical-module swaps need an event list - the CL31 swap in 6.3 shows the consequence).
33. Sect. 5.1.1 window selection: the composite score prefers the window with the lowest signal-to-molecular ratio ("near-unity scattering ratio", "relative to the cleanest window"). With noisy windows this selects negative noise excursions and biases C_L low (selection of minima). Has this been tested with synthetic noise? Give the magnitude for a CL61 at 3-6 km.
34. Sect. 5.1.2 / App. 4: the single droplet size 5.5 um (from three continental sites) is applied network-wide; the figure title itself says marine stratocumulus r_e ~ 8-13 um. For a 0.83 mrad CL31 at a coastal/marine site (Keflavik, Mace Head, Bonaire) eta at 1-2 km changes by several %. Quantify or state as a limitation; give the MS uncertainty budget (r_e IQR 4.5-7 um).
35. Sect. 5.1.2: "a NIGHT without a matching CAMS profile is left un-calibrated" - the cloud calibration uses day and night profiles (Fig. 4 spans 0-24 UTC). Say "day".
36. Sect. 6.3 Palaiseau/Payerne: the 910 -> 1064 nm conversion with Angstrom exponent 1 is not given an uncertainty: beta_aer(1064)/beta_aer(910) = (1064/910)^-A = 0.856 (A = 1), 0.925 (A = 0.5), 0.79 (A = 1.5), i.e. +/-8 % on the aerosol part. State it next to the "within a few per cent" claims.
37. Sect. 6.3 Amsterdam: unit D is the reference "arbitrarily"; the repository's own work notes record an optical-module swap on the Schiphol D unit - if it falls inside Jan 2025-Oct 2026, the reference changed during the comparison. Verify and state.
38. Sect. 6.3 conclusion "within ~5 % ... between identical instruments (A, C, D)" - three of four; say so (B is 20 % off).
39. Sect. 4.3: the fixed 25 % relative uncertainty of L2 is "empirical"; the paper's own results (5 % calibration, 20-100 % free-tropospheric artefacts) could replace it or at least justify it. A referee will ask for an uncertainty budget table for beta_att (Rayleigh nightly 2 sigma, Kalman, cloud per-profile spread, S_liq +/-0.8 sr, WV (CAMS humidity), MS (r_e), S_aer bounds, overlap, dark).

Suggest a check with a line near 900-1000 nm.
